title: Was one Billion Americans written too early? 
alias: Did MattYglesias write One Billion Americans too soon
date: 3/24/2024
---

In [One Billion Americans: The Case for Thinking Bigger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Billion_Americans), journalist Matthew Yglesias argues that the United States could and should have way more citizens. 

Published in September 2020, I wonder if Yglesias should have waited another decade? Was One Billion Americans published too soon? 

Since then:
- On housing, YIMBYism is having a moment —  [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/03/09/business/economy/yimby-housing-conference.html) ran a piece on “The Surprising Left-Right Alliance” to build more housing
- On climate, optimism is increasing — [the head of the UN’s IPCC says](https://amp.dw.com/en/climate-change-do-not-overstate-15-degrees-threat/a-66386523) “we should not despair and fall into a state of shock” if global warming of 1.5C happens
- For investment, there’s energy — venture capitalists say “[It’s Time to Build ](https://a16z.com/its-time-to-build/)(April 2020 but it takes a while to finish a book)
- With geopolitics, it’s more top of mind — “Americans are increasingly worried about China-Taiwan tensions” [according to Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/03/29/americans-are-increasingly-worried-about-china-taiwan-tensions/)
- For pro-natalism, it’s mainstream — earning a series on [Ezra Klein’s podcast](https://www.nytimes.com/column/ezra-klein-podcast)

On the one hand, this makes Yglesias look quite prescient. But a book that argues for a set of policy positions might be most impactful closer to the peak popularity of those ideas.
