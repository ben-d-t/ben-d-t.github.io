---
title: The essays about progress that I share the most
date: 1/31/2025
---

I'm explaining my thoughts on [[economic-progress|progress]] to friends and family more often recently. I'm not that concise. I keep sending some links afterwards. So I wanted to put together a list of my favorite writing about progress. These are some of the essays that have most influenced my thinking. 

1: [The world is awful. The world is much better. The world can be much better. - Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/much-better-awful-can-be-better) by Max Roser. My top recommendation and possibly the link I share the most. Also an example of how [[dense ideas|compressing the best ideas is hard]].

2: [Why do we need a NEW philosophy of progress?](https://blog.rootsofprogress.org/why-a-new-philosophy-of-progress) by Jason Crawford. I find myself sharing this frequently when someone says "but what about ---"

3: [Progress Is a Policy Choice | IFP](https://ifp.org/progress-is-a-policy-choice/) by Alec Stapp and Caleb Watney. You get the gist. One of the best taglines in progress studies. 

4: [The housing theory of everything - Works in Progress](https://worksinprogress.co/issue/the-housing-theory-of-everything/) by John Myers, Sam Bowman, and Ben Southwood. Illustrates my slippery slope from interest in housing economics into progress studies. 

5: [What Do You Do With an Idea?](https://www.notboring.co/p/what-do-you-do-with-an-idea) by Packy McCormick. I'm not sure this idea is correct but it's probably the best and most accessible *synthesis* of why stagnation happened and what can be done about it -- also, this essay, inspired by the [Progress Conference 2024](https://rootsofprogress.org/conference/) is a great example of the value of convening/events. 

6: [Technological stagnation: Why I came around](https://blog.rootsofprogress.org/technological-stagnation) by Jason Crawford. A solid primer on "the great stagnation" and the case for each side. Not everyone has bumped into [WTF Happened In 1971?](https://wtfhappenedin1971.com)

7: [Climate optimism of the will](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/climate-optimism-of-the-will) by Noah Smith. I happened to read this at a time I was deep in modeling the costs of the energy transition. This is a big part of [[climate|Why I'm not worried about climate change]] -- but we still need to "fight and win". 

8: [Why pessimism sounds smart](https://blog.rootsofprogress.org/why-pessimism-sounds-smart) by Jason Crawford. I share this when I get into a meta-argument about whether optimism is realistic or not. 

9: [Can growth continue?](https://blog.rootsofprogress.org/can-growth-continue-ignite-talk) by Jason Crawford. "Now, that’s a problem for another time. But note that in five minutes we’ve gone from worrying about overpopulation to underpopulation. That’s because we’ve traded a scarcity mindset, where growth is limited by resources, for an abundance mindset, where it is limited only by our ingenuity."

10: [We Need a New Science of Progress - The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/science/archive/2019/07/we-need-new-science-progress/594946/) by Patrick Collison and Tyler Cowen. An essay that started a movement (though of course many people were working on these questions already).
